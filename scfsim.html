Credit risk propagation in supply chain finance, as an experiment you can run

SCFSim is an open-source agent-based simulator of default cascades in multi-tier supply chain finance networks — one core enterprise, tiered suppliers, banks — where the financing technology is a controllable experimental variable. The headline, and the condition it carries

Deep-tier financing halves the cascade — in the chains it is offered to

Switching on the blockchain scenario — receivable visibility across all tiers, verification haircut 25% → 5%, fraud 6% → 0.5% — cuts the mean final default share from 55.6% to 26.5% on the paper's stressed 56-firm network. The software also ships the switch that qualifies this result: pay suppliers on the same one-period terms as their customers (firm.payables_delay = 1) and the working-capital gap that receivables financing bridges largely closes — the gap between scenarios collapses from 29 to 2 points, though systemic events still fall by a third. The headline is therefore a statement about chains in which suppliers are paid later than they pay, and every document in the repository says so.

The model in one paragraph

Settlement → orders → production → financing → default

Each period, receivables booked one trade-credit cycle earlier are settled (defaulted buyers pay only a recovery rate), the core enterprise draws stochastic demand which cascades upstream through input shares, and firms deliver against their order book up to remaining supply capacity. A firm short of cash requests receivables financing from its house bank; eligible collateral depends on how deep the financing visibility of confirmed core payables reaches — tier 1 in traditional SCF, effectively unbounded on a blockchain platform — minus a verification haircut and an expected-fraud discount. Banks accumulate losses and tighten credit as capital erodes; a failed bank stops lending. Firms that remain illiquid default, hitting their buyers (supply disruption), their suppliers (demand contraction, counterparty losses) and their bank (credit crunch). Four channels, two ablation designs

The rank order of the channels reverses — the coupling is not decorative

Acting alone, counterparty losses dominate and supply disruption does almost nothing. Removed from the coupled model, supply disruption is the largest contributor: it needs the other channels to generate the failures it then amplifies, so a one-at-a-time design cannot see it. The credit crunch is genuinely second-order in SCF — receivables lending is self-liquidating and capped by collateral — and only binds as bank capitalisation falls.

Verification

Checked against answers computed without simulating

A simulator of this kind has no reference output, so the engine verifies itself in five layers — and every layer is itself demonstrated to work by re-injecting a failure it must catch. A guard that cannot catch the bug it was written for is not a guard.

LayerWhat it assertsHow its effectiveness is shown
Reachability bounds With one trade-graph channel active, the attributable cascade lies inside the descendants (supply) or ancestors (demand) of the seed set — a graph-theoretic bound. Re-checked over thirty random topologies and parameterisations per direction.
Accounting identities Exposure equals the borrower's principal; write-offs never exceed lending net of recovery; drawings equal supply. Checked every period in strict mode. Eleven mutation tests corrupt the ledgers; each corruption must be caught.
Economic properties Deliveries bounded by order book × capacity; bank capital scaled to its own credit book; a drawing collateralised when made. Eleven signed comparative statics. Both historical specification errors are re-injected; each must fire its check.
Metamorphic relations Rescaling the monetary unit, permuting firm labels and shortening the horizon must leave outcomes unchanged. Each relation is also run against a deliberately faulted engine, where it must fail.
Differential testing On a single linear chain the engine reproduces an independently written reference implementation to within 10⁻⁹ — cash, loans, defaults, write-offs, period by period. Ten injected faults (collateral +2%, sales +10⁻⁴, …) must each break agreement.

What the stack cannot tell you

Every layer tests a property somebody wrote down in advance. The defects found since v0.9 did not come from the stack — each came from using the model in a new way: switching an assumption exposed the headline's condition, a tier-1 seed exposed a channel outside the differential comparison, a user-supplied network exposed two engine defects. The one use not yet made is independent review by a supply chain finance expert; a structured 90-minute review form exists for exactly that gap, and its first question is answered before reading the specification, so the reviewer's list is not anchored on ours.

Limitations, ranked by impact

What the numbers are not

  1. Immediate payables by default. Suppliers are paid on delivery while their customers pay on terms — the widest working-capital gap. The switch that closes it ships in the box and collapses the headline gap (see above). Ranked first.
  2. No calibration. All parameters are illustrative; the model reproduces qualitative mechanisms, and absolute magnitudes are not forecasts.
  3. Financing terms do not enter the firm's decision. Lender-based risk pricing is a switch and, measured, changes results by under one point — quantity rationing dominates. Borrower-based pricing and any demand response to price are not modelled.
  4. Irreversible distress. No recovery, no re-sourcing, no rebuilt capacity: cascades are upper bounds relative to a chain with active mitigation.
  5. Share-weighted settlement, not invoice-level; idiosyncratic invoice exposure is not represented.
  6. A single final customer. The core enterprise anchors all demand; its default (also a switch) ends the chain by construction.

Every assumption is documented and ranked in docs/FINANCIAL_SPEC.md.

Reproduce

Every number on this page, from three commands

Install

git clone https://github.com/zouxuangit/scfsim
cd scfsim
pip install -e ".[dev]"      # Python ≥ 3.9; numpy, networkx, matplotlib

Run the tests that pin the paper

MPLBACKEND=Agg pytest tests/ -q   # 163 tests; the slow layer asserts Table 5
                                  # and the channel decomposition at printed precision

Re-run the experiments

python examples/blockchain_switch.py            # Table 5, both halves (~30 s)
python examples/channels_and_sensitivity.py     # Fig. 2 (~5 min; --quick for seconds)
python examples/custom_network.py               # bring-your-own-network demo

CI additionally re-runs the suite with every dependency pinned to the floor of its declared range (numpy 1.22.4, networkx 2.8, matplotlib 3.5.3), where the headline numbers are asserted to reproduce bit-for-bit. docs/REPRODUCTION.md maps every figure in the paper to the console line that produces it. The interactive panels on this page are driven by data computed by the released engine with the paper's own scenario functions and seeds.
